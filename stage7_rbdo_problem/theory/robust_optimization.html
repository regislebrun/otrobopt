Robust optimization measures¶

This page is the catalogue of the robustness and reliability measures provided by otrobopt. It states the definition, the output dimension and the intended use of each measure, together with the discretization and solver interfaces. The mathematical contract, the invariance theorem and the canonical transformation are stated in Pushforward, invariance and canonicalization; this page complements it with the measure-by-measure reference.

Robustness measures¶

Robustness measures quantify the performance of a design under uncertainty. They are used as the objective, and therefore must be scalar-valued. RobustOptimizationProblem rejects a vector-valued robust objective.

Reliability measures¶

Reliability measures quantify the feasibility of a design under uncertainty. They are used as constraints. The otrobopt convention is for an inequality constraint, so a reliability requirement must be expressed with GreaterOrEqual.

Aggregated measure¶

AggregatedMeasure concatenates the outputs of several measures into a single vector-valued measure,

It is used internally by RobustOptimizationProblem to assemble the objective and the constraints into one evaluation. The children share the same distribution and the same parameter vector. A mixed set of distributions is rejected by the problem and by the canonicalizer, including inside an aggregate.

SequentialMonteCarloRobustAlgorithm iteratively refines the discretization and re-optimizes until convergence. The algorithm is

The main aspects are the following.

  • Sample size. New samples are added at each iteration to improve the accuracy of the discretization. The increment is configurable; the default adds new samples.

  • Multi-start. On the first iteration an optional multi-start search over the bounds, driven by a Sobol low-discrepancy sequence, provides a promising starting point.

  • Convergence. The algorithm stops when the change of the design point falls below the absolute error tolerance.

The sequential approach avoids committing to a fixed discretization level before the optimization: the approximation improves as the optimization progresses, which yields efficient and robust convergence.
